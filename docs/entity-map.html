Systems review

Entity Map: what owns what

Four systems, one business. This fixes the canonical owner for every noun so the bakery can open without three copies of the truth.

4 Aug 2026 Priority: bakery opens Counts pulled from live prod

The verdict

You do not have four apps that should become one. You have one coordination layer, two engines, and one publishing platform that is no longer on the critical path.

The problem worth fixing is not the number of systems. It is that Organization and Person exist three times, and Product — the actual business object — exists canonically nowhere. Everything else is working.

square What you sell, and for how much System of record for catalog, price, and every sale. Never duplicate a price out of it.
daily-baker What things cost to make Recipes, ingredients, FIFO inventory, production, margin. The cost twin of the Square catalog.
neemee Who you deal with, what you owe Projects, tasks, notes, organizations, people. Already the system you actually run the buildout from.
grain-exchange Public content and directory Multi-tenant publishing platform. Real, but not on the path to opening. Freeze it.

Two things to fix before Product gets any rows

A

Product is modeled at the wrong grain

Square has 70 items but 99 variations, and the variation is what carries SKU and price. A large loaf and a small loaf have different yields, different costs, and different packaging. Modeling Product against the item collapses that.

Point Product at the Square variation, and join on squareVariationId — the catalog object ID, not SKU. SKU covers only 83 of 99 variations and you can edit it by hand in the Square dashboard, which makes it a bad key.

B

Product.recipeId is required, but you resell flour

The schema declares recipeId String with a Restrict relation, so every product must have a recipe. Square is already selling Rouge de Bordeaux, Yecora Rojo, and Turkey Red stone ground whole wheat flour, none of which is a recipe.

Make recipeId nullable and add a kind — made versus resold. Resold products cost what you paid, and their margin comes from the purchase price, not a bill of materials.

Both are free to change right now: the products table is empty. After the first import they become migrations.

Sequence

  1. Fix the Product schema
    Variation grain, nullable recipe, made/resold kind, squareVariationId unique.
    Before any data
  2. Import 99 Square variations as product shells
    Name, SKU, price, category, and the Square ID. No costs yet.
    Same session
  3. Attach recipes to what you bake
    46 recipes against ~70 made variations. The unmatched ones are the honest gap list.
    Next
  4. Populate supplies and packaging
    Currently zero, so supplyCost is structurally zero and every margin reads high.
    Before opening
  5. Add neemeeOrgId to Vendor
    7 vendors against 14 organizations. Reconcile by hand once; it never gets cheaper than this.
    Any time
  6. Freeze grain-exchange
    No new features. Farmers-market vendor management is a future product, not a dependency of opening.
    Decision, not work

Deliberately not doing

  • Not merging databases. Three Postgres instances with different owners and lifecycles is correct. Foreign keys across the boundary are enough.
  • Not building a unifying app. The reliable outcome of that move is four systems instead of three.
  • Not integrating grain-exchange. Its 140 tables encode a publishing platform. Wiring it to bakery operations imports a decade of accumulated complexity to solve a problem you don't have.
  • Not pruning grain-exchange yet. Nearly every model has a controller, so dead code can't be identified statically — it needs production row counts and traffic. Worth doing once the bakery is open, not before.
